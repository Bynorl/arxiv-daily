Deep Learning for Atmospheric Turbulence Mitigation: A Comparison Between GANs and Transformers

Deep Learning for Atmospheric Turbulence Mitigation: A Comparison Between GANs and Transformers

Juan S. Carrillo、Jérôme Gilles

论文页面 PDF HTML 版本 暂无开源

Atmospheric turbulence creates complex spatial and temporal distortions that significantly reduce image quality. This work compares two deep learning methods for turbulence reduction: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Transformer-based models. Both methods are trained and tested on the SOTIS dataset, which includes multi-frame sequences under different turbulence conditions with ground truth references. Our results show an important finding: GAN-based models perform better in severely degraded, low-quality situations, while Transformer-based models achieve better results in moderate to high-quality settings, especially in correcting geometric distortions. We also enhance the Turbulence Mitigation Transformer (TMT) by introducing a refined training strategy, an augmented input design that includes a temporally averaged frame as prior structural information, and a modified loss function that incorporates the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). These improvements lead to consistent performance gains across various turbulence conditions. Overall, this study provides an in-depth evaluation of deep learning methods for turbulence reduction and offers practical guidance for selecting models in different imaging environments.

High-resolution retinal matrix imaging via computational motion and aberration correction

High-resolution retinal matrix imaging via computational motion and aberration correction

Aymeric Melt、Léo Puyo、Victor Barolle、Claude Boccara、Gereon Hüttmann 等

论文页面 PDF HTML 版本 暂无开源

High-resolution optical imaging of the human retina is essential for diagnosing ophthalmic and neurodegenerative diseases. While adaptive optics combined with coherence gating can resolve retinal structures at the cellular scale, it requires complex, costly, and cumbersome hardware to physically correct for ocular aberrations and motion. Matrix imaging offers a powerful digital alternative by leveraging wave linearity to undo aberrations and multiple scattering; however, its in vivo deployment has been hindered by continuous, involuntary eye movements that destroy phase coherence during acquisition. Here, we overcome this fundamental barrier and demonstrate the first in vivo reflection matrix imaging of the human retina using an ultra-simplified optical setup without physical correctors. We introduce a fully computational framework that tracks and compensates for multi-axial ocular motion directly from raw interferometric data, thereby restoring the phase coherence of a multi-spectral distortion matrix. This enables local, high-order aberration correction to achieve diffraction-limited, volumetric imaging of retinal tissues. By shifting the hardware burden to physics-guided numerical computation, this paradigm opens the way for accessible, non-invasive, and high-resolution digital ophthalmology.

Per-Bundle, Pre-Reconstruction Inversion for Temporal CT: Flux-Compensation Design and Multi-Patient Generalization

Per-Bundle, Pre-Reconstruction Inversion for Temporal CT: Flux-Compensation Design and Multi-Patient Generalization

Guy M Besson

论文页面 PDF HTML 版本 暂无开源

Paper 1 established the per-bundle statistical limits of Temporal CT (TCT) and benchmarked a learned prior on a single patient at a uniform incident count $N_0$. This paper lifts both restrictions. SNN2, a Gauss-Newton estimator, converges to the per-bundle Poisson maximum-likelihood solution and is the prior-free baseline. N$_0$FC, a bow-tie and tube-current-modulation co-design, compresses within-bundle photon-flux dynamic range. V8c is a neural-network residual corrector trained on a 19-patient corpus stratified by water-equivalent diameter (WED). T2bp, a per-patient empirical-Bayes post-processor, combines per-bundle Poisson likelihoods with a prior built from the patient's own bundles; it requires no training, so it cannot inherit a mismatched training prior. Results use Paper 1's bias-charged Dose Inflation Factor (DIF), extended to per-ray budgets. V8c beats SNN2 on in-distribution patients under baseline acquisition but fails out of distribution. Composed with SNN2, T2bp matches or beats T2bp$\circ$V8c on three of four N$_0$FC test cells (WED 207-342 mm), with the largest gains on the high-WED and out-of-distribution patients, and trails by 3% on the fourth. On 23 non-test corpus volumes, N$_0$FC with closed-loop tube-current modulation lowers the per-volume median middle-path Cramer-Rao floor, as a DIF, from 23-48 under baseline acquisition to 3.3-6.3, near the equal-attenuation value 13/3; T2bp$\circ$SNN2 reaches this floor without training, its mean squared error 0.74-1.26 times the floor (median 0.89) over the bulk of each volume. Acquisition co-design removes most of the attenuation-contrast penalty, and a training-free estimator attains the resulting limit. The remaining three- to six-fold gap to dose parity with single-source CT, and the high-attenuation tail, are the target of inter-bundle priors in later work.

Perceptual Characterization of Scan-induced Artifacts in Dynamic 3D Meshes

Perceptual Characterization of Scan-induced Artifacts in Dynamic 3D Meshes

Huong Hoang、Truong Nguyen、Pamela Cosman

论文页面 PDF HTML 版本 暂无开源

Dynamic meshes reconstructed from real-world scenes often exhibit scan-induced artifacts such as surface jitter and missing geometry, which strongly impact perceived visual quality. Objective assessment remains challenging due to the absence of artifact-free ground truth and limited perceptual understanding of artifact interactions. This paper presents a systematic and controlled perceptual study of scan-induced artifacts in dynamic meshes, focusing on surface jitter and holes. To enable systematic analysis, we introduce a physically grounded artifact synthesis framework in which holes are synthesized using a reconstruction uncertainty model derived from multi-view geometry. The study reveals asymmetric cross-artifact interactions, non-additive integration, and motion-dependent temporal masking. Based on these findings, we propose a compact, perceptually grounded no-reference quality assessment framework that encodes the observed perceptual mechanisms.

Personalising Deformable Image Registration with Clinical Context and Deformation History for Adaptive Radiotherapy

Personalising Deformable Image Registration with Clinical Context and Deformation History for Adaptive Radiotherapy

Caiwen Jiang、Songlin Zhao、Yuzhen Ding、Jia Mi、Samir H. Patel 等

论文页面 PDF HTML 版本 暂无开源

Adaptive radiotherapy must account for anatomical changes during treatment, but doing so depends on reliably mapping targets, organs at risk and dose across serial images. Existing registration methods treat each image pair independently, without using radiotherapy-specific context or the patient's preceding anatomical trajectory. We developed Clinical-context-Aware and Patient-adaptive Deformable Image Registration (CAP-DIR), integrating source contours, dose, clinical text and prior-registration features. We evaluated 16,361 CT examinations from 3,473 patients across four institutional cohorts and five public benchmarks. CAP-DIR increased internal-test organ-at-risk-region normalized cross-correlation from 84.76% to 96.92% relative to the strongest learning-based comparator, with low deformation folding, and remained superior across seven external cohorts. In a blinded review of 20 longitudinal cases, clinically acceptable contours increased from 45.0% to 82.5% for clinical target volumes and from 22.5% to 62.5% for organs at risk, while estimated editing time decreased. In a controlled benchmark, CAP-DIR reduced global dose-mapping error by 34.5% relative to the strongest comparator. These findings show that clinically informed, longitudinal registration can improve contour usability and dose mapping for adaptive radiotherapy.

DoReMe: Dual optimization of Reconstruction and Motion estimation for self-navigated free-breathing abdominal MRI using Gaussian Splatting

DoReMe: Dual optimization of Reconstruction and Motion estimation for self-navigated free-breathing abdominal MRI using Gaussian Splatting

Majd Helo、Dominik Nickel、Thomas Kuestner

论文页面 PDF HTML 版本 暂无开源

Purpose: Free-breathing abdominal MRI is limited by respiratory motion, which introduces image artifacts and k-space inconsistencies. Existing reconstruction approaches often depend on pre-estimated motion fields or external respiratory surrogate signals. We propose a coupled reconstruction-motion estimation algorithm for self-navigated free-breathing acquisition, in which motion is represented by compact Gaussian primitives and jointly refined with the image estimates during iterative reconstruction. Methods: An iterative reconstruction that alternates between data consistency updates and motion modelling was proposed. Respiratory motion was represented using Gaussian primitives. A Gaussian splatting was integrated into the reconstruction algorithm. The method was evaluated on self-navigated free-breathing Cartesian gradient-echo (GRE) acquisition in four healthy volunteers, including retrospective scan shortening experiments, and was compared with regularized compressed sensing (CS) reconstruction. Results: Respiratory motion states were reconstructed in approximately 1.5 min. Compared with CS, the proposed method improved visual image quality, particularly in coronal liver views, and better preserved anatomical structures in retrospectively shortened acquisitions. These findings provide proof of concept for the proposed method. Conclusion: The proposed coupled reconstruction-motion estimation algorithm improved visual image quality compared with CS alone. Gaussian primitives provided a compact and sufficiently flexible representation of nonrigid respiratory motion with practical runtime, supporting further scanner integration and validation in larger cohorts.

Focus, Align, and Diffuse: Time-Series-Aware Keyframe Diffusion for Cardiac Dynamic Synthesis from Sparse Observations

Focus, Align, and Diffuse: Time-Series-Aware Keyframe Diffusion for Cardiac Dynamic Synthesis from Sparse Observations

Junkai Liu、Haofan Wu、Nay Aung、Joao A. C. Lima、Steffen E. Petersen 等

论文页面 PDF HTML 版本 暂无开源

Dense cardiac dynamics are essential for assessing cardiac function, yet in practice clinicians often observe only extremely sparse measurements. However, recovering full dynamics from such sparsity is a highly ill-posed problem due to the lack of temporal cues. While auxiliary time-series signals like ECG provide physiological guidance, effectively exploiting them is challenging due to the cross-modal representation gap and phase misalignment. In this work, we present CardioFAD, a Focus--Align--Diffuse paradigm that reformulates sparse cardiac dynamic synthesis as a recoverability--alignability anchor discovery problem. Our core insight is that resolving extreme temporal ambiguity requires a compact set of anchor states that are both sufficient to recover the visual trajectory and reliable for ECG--visual alignment. We therefore learn motion-salient keyframes under two complementary principles: trajectory sufficiency, which preserves trajectory-critical intra-modal motion content, and anchor alignment, which promotes consistent inter-modal correspondence at these anchors. We implement the Focus and Align modules optimized by the two aspects. Diffuse then performs two-stage generation by first synthesizing keyframes and subsequently interpolating the remaining frames. Extensive experiments on cardiac MRI and echocardiography videos demonstrate superior visual quality, temporal coherence, and improved functional consistency under the extreme one-shot regime.

A$^3$PreCore: Robust Rigid Registration for Native FLAIR-Space Brain-Mask Transfer

A$^3$PreCore: Robust Rigid Registration for Native FLAIR-Space Brain-Mask Transfer

Elmira Mirzabeigi、Zahra Khalilzad、Taylor Snowden-Richardson、Jesus Eduardo Lugo Arce、Jodie R. Gawryluk 等

论文页面 PDF HTML 版本 暂无开源

Reliable brain-mask transfer into native fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) space can fail when T1-to-FLAIR rigid registration is unstable. We developed A$^3$PreCore, a six-degree-of-freedom rigid-registration strategy that aligns the full bias-corrected T1-weighted image to native FLAIR using normalized mutual information while preserving the established T1-space anatomical mask. We also introduced extended quantitative quality control (E-QC), which characterizes mask extent, field-of-view-relative position, FLAIR intensity support, boundary support, inferior morphology, and pairwise geometric disagreement. In 78 participants with paired T1-weighted and FLAIR imaging, A$^3$PreCore was compared with fsl_anat and the Brain Extraction Tool (BET). Gross rigid-body misregistration occurred in 21/78 fsl_anat outputs (26.9%) and 0/78 A$^3$PreCore outputs (exact two-sided McNemar p=9.54 x 10^-7). Among 57 participants without gross fsl_anat misregistration, A$^3$PreCore preserved close agreement (median Dice=0.993; median paired Delta NMI=-0.00007, p=0.429). A$^3$PreCore remained broadly concordant with BET (median Dice=0.955), with less inferior and extracranial extension in representative comparisons. Method-blinded neuroscientists ranked A$^3$PreCore first in 35/40 participants (87.5%). E-QC additionally exposed severe orientation-related disagreement not evident from global descriptors or centroid displacement alone. These findings support A$^3$PreCore as a robust native-FLAIR brain-mask transfer strategy and E-QC as a complementary framework for multidimensional mask characterization and participant-specific geometric quality control.
